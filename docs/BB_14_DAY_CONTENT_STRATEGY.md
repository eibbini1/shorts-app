# Billionaire Bites: 14-day Short strategy

Promise: The world's most outrageous food — what it costs, why it is rare,
and whether it is worth it. Target one Short a day once we have release-ready
packs. Do not fill empty slots with repeat stories or previews.

Target 25–32 seconds, roughly 65–75 spoken words, one idea and one payoff.
Open on food or action in frame one. Use quick cuts when new information
arrives; let a good texture shot breathe. Give on-screen prices a date,
currency, and unit. Show a map only when it clarifies place or jurisdiction.
End on a visual loop or a short question viewers can answer in one word.

## Five production sketches

1. **The $36,500 melon — auction vs. shopping basket.** 0–2: two boxed
   melons and ¥5.8M FOR THE PAIR. 2–9: date the May 2026 opening auction
   in Sapporo. 9–20: contrast a ceremonial auction bid with a separately
   sourced ordinary retail example; use gift wrapping, rind and sliced fruit.
   20–30: reveal why the auction price is exceptional; ask GIFT or BREAKFAST.
   BB already covered this exact auction. Hold unless the retail comparison
   and all visuals are new. $36,500 is a rounded historical conversion for
   two whole melons, not the price of a slice.

2. **Three food rules that aren't the same ban.** 0–2: three close-ups under
   SALE / PRODUCTION / IMPORT. 2–10: show the EU market rule for products
   from specified poisonous fish families. 10–18: distinguish the UK's
   foie-gras force-feeding production restriction from a sales ban.
   18–26: use a third jurisdiction-specific example only after verifying
   current law and enforcement scope; casu marzu is a research candidate,
   not an approved blanket EU/US claim. 26–30: ask which distinction was
   most surprising. Hold the prior BB list format unless the examples,
   map graphics, and explanation are distinct.

3. **Why a truffle hunter brings a dog.** 0–2: dog stops and sniffs; show an
   earthy lump under the soil. 2–10: forest and root-level detail establish
   the hunt. 10–20: follow the scent cue to the brush-off. 20–28: reveal the
   white-truffle cross-section, then shave it onto a warm dish. Ask KEEP or
   COOK. Verify the dog/harvesting details with a primary source before
   recording. A historical auction is a separate fact, not evidence that
   ordinary truffles cost more than gold. BB covered the dirt/gold headline:
   use this only as a new hunt-to-plate story with new footage.

4. **Pick one: $1 noodles or a $10,000 menu.** 0–2: two bowls, PICK ONE.
   2–10: glossy noodles lifted through steam. 10–20: private tasting-menu
   presentation with a single appetizing detail at each cut. 20–27: set
   up comfort versus spectacle. 27–30: ask NOODLES or TASTING. Label the
   comparison hypothetical unless we verify both prices, place and date.
   Don't promise a long video unless it is actually planned. Make a new
   scenario and new footage rather than reusing BB's previous menu edit.

5. **How red saffron turns a pan gold.** 0–2: red threads land beside golden
   risotto; caption RED THREADS, GOLD RICE. 2–10: show crocus stigmas and
   hand preparation. 10–21: close-up of threads blooming in warm liquid,
   then pour it into the pan. 21–30: spoon lift with visible glossy grains;
   loop to a falling thread or ask SAFFRON or GOLD. Use the PDO source for
   the San Gimignano production angle. Drop the more-expensive-than-gold
   claim unless like-for-like, dated prices actually support it.

## Slots 6–14: reserve concepts, not scheduled

6. Luxury fruit: grade a different fruit side by side; verify actual price.
7. Why a rare dish takes hours to prepare; select a sourced dish.
8. Pick one: caviar or Wagyu; show original serving shots.
9. One cheese rule in one jurisdiction; no duplicate of the earlier list.
10. Gift fruit: contrast packaging and grading for a different variety.
11. High-priced coffee: contrast processing methods; avoid the existing
    elephant-coffee story unless it is a materially new production.
12. A real $10 plate versus a verified luxury counterpart, different from 4.
13. One food-safety rule explained with source and jurisdiction, no stunts.
14. One historic food auction record, scoped to its category and date.

## Measure

Capture 24 h and 72 h snapshots, then review the 14-day batch. Compare
similar traffic sources and reporting age. Use Stayed to watch for the
opening, average percentage viewed and retention at the reveal for pacing,
subscribers per 1,000 engaged views for conversion, and viewer comments
for prompt comparisons. CTR is a registered-thumbnail-impression metric,
so use it for relevant surfaces, not as the Shorts Feed swipe rate.
A 21-second average is a duration, not a percentage; 1.95%, 2%, and 5.7 are
hypotheses or baselines only if their scope and denominator match the data.
Scale the two strongest formats after enough comparable evidence, with
fresh topics and footage each time.
