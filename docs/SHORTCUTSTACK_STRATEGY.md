## Positioning and channel packaging

Promise: “Practical AI shortcuts for real work—with the result shown on screen.” Initial audience: freelancers and office workers handling briefs, documents, emails and repetitive tasks. Use one language and one clear audience for the next 30 days.

Keep ShortcutStack as the channel name. Suggested banner: “AI shortcuts for real work” with a smaller line, “Clear demos. Useful results.” Use a simple stacked-sheet or shortcut symbol that remains recognizable at avatar size. About copy: “ShortcutStack tests practical AI workflows for everyday work. Learn to turn messy documents, emails and repetitive tasks into clear next steps. Every tutorial shows the workflow, the result, and the limits. Byte & Max bring the questions; real demonstrations bring the proof.”

Organize the homepage around Start Here, Document Shortcuts, Everyday AI Fixes and Full Workflow Tutorials. Pin a useful workflow demonstration when available. Keep existing relevant videos while reviewing them; do not delete the catalogue simply because views are low.

## Content mix

Start with five Shorts and one 5–8 minute tutorial per week for four weeks, provided every upload passes factual and visual review. If quality slips, reduce to three Shorts and one tutorial. More available time should improve testing and proof rather than inflate upload volume.

Allocate roughly 60% to document and source-based workflows, 30% to concrete work problems such as email and screenshots, and 10% to carefully tested experiments. Treat these as starting allocations. Avoid broad tool lists, generic AI news and unsupported savings claims. Recheck product features, free-tier limits and interface labels immediately before recording.

Each Short solves one task and supplies one useful result. Each long tutorial completes a larger task with original examples, setup, mistakes, verification and limitations. Link a relevant Short to its full tutorial using available YouTube related-video features. Do not assume Shorts viewers will convert; measure cross-format viewers and tutorial traffic.

Byte & Max should initially be hosts within this productivity promise. Use a short problem setup and a concise reaction, with real screen recordings carrying most of the instructional sequence. Do not turn the channel into unrelated children's stories without a separate audience decision.

## Episode 01 refinement

The reference images establish warm workshop lighting, mustard clothing, a white robot and cyan accents. The supplied export instead uses flat 2D artwork. Both can work, but choose a repeatable visual language; reserve cinematic references for occasional hero assets until production economics are proven.

The existing opening “30 pages. One question.” creates tension. Make the question concrete immediately: “What is the client actually asking us to deliver?” The lower caption zone and upper branding consume space; enlarge the demonstrated tool and remove duplicate headings. Sampled frames show Max cropped at the right edge during close-ups; reframe deliberately. The workflow card is explicitly illustrative: replace it with a real capture before promising an executable walkthrough.

Proposed 30-second cut:

1. 0–2s: Show messy brief and the question “WHAT'S DUE?” Max: “Thirty pages. What do I actually owe this client?”
2. 2–5s: Byte: “Let's find the deliverables—and check the sources.” Show a real, fictional client brief ready to upload.
3. 5–16s: Demonstrate the current tool interface. Narration: “Add a brief you're allowed to share. Ask: List each deliverable and deadline. Cite the source for each. If something is missing, say so.”
4. 16–25s: Show the actual result, open one citation and compare it with the document. Narration: “Open the citation and check it. If the deadline isn't stated, ask the client.”
5. 25–30s: Max: “So I still have to read?” Byte: “Just the parts that matter.” End on the verified deliverables, with a small “Follow for practical AI shortcuts.”

This is a production draft. Record the exact workflow first, then adjust wording to what it actually does. Do not fabricate a successful tool response.

Title candidates: “Find the deliverables hidden in a client brief” and “30-page client brief? Find what’s actually due”. Opening frame: large readable brief, one highlighted deliverable, minimal text. Avoid implying AI never guesses.

## Design and sound system

Shorts: 1080×1920. Keep instructional text roughly within x=90–900 and y=220–1450 as a starting layout, then check real app overlays on a phone. Caption size around 52–64 px, at most two short lines, strong contrast, sentence case. These are production starting points, not official safe-area guarantees. Show one interaction at a time; crop and zoom the UI rather than shrinking an entire desktop.

Use charcoal/teal neutral surfaces, warm white, mustard for Max and cyan for Byte. Use accents for focus, not alternating rainbow captions. Reuse Poppins already present in assets/fonts. Keep branding small. Long-video thumbnails: one outcome, one main subject, two to four readable words; e.g. “WHAT'S DUE?” against a visibly messy brief. Build distinct thumbnails around the specific problem.

Speech must be intelligible on a phone speaker. Keep music below narration; remove it where it obscures instructions. Listen to the full final render and inspect caption timing, pronunciation, clipping and dead air before release.

The D-drive app contains ElevenLabs generation and cached narration support. MockTTSProvider produces a tone and is unsuitable for narration. Local voice files or another engine may exist elsewhere; their identity and commercial permissions still need verification. Reuse audio only for the exact matching script. A suggested budget does not authorize paid synthesis.

## First four weeks

Week 1: Collect Studio baseline; record one complete document workflow. Publish Shorts on extracting deliverables, finding unstated deadlines, checking citations, rewriting one vague request, and one Byte & Max version of the same audience problem with a distinct example. Long tutorial: “Turn a messy client brief into a checked action plan.”

Week 2: Test three source-based document questions and two screenshot troubleshooting tasks. Compare direct result openings with problem openings using different but comparable examples. Long tutorial: a complete document-to-action workflow with an intentional missing fact and a verification step.

Week 3: Expand the strongest problem cluster with three fresh use cases. Produce two email-workflow Shorts if actual tools and privacy conditions support the examples. Long tutorial: turn a sample client email into a response and task list, with manual verification. Avoid treating one isolated spike as proof.

Week 4: Make three sequels addressing viewer questions and two tests of the weakest specific production assumption. Publish a deeper tutorial in the better-supported cluster. Review the full month at equal upload ages and choose the next month's mix.

First ten briefs, in priority order:

1. Find deliverables in a long brief. Proof: source passage beside extracted task.
2. Find what the brief forgot. Proof: missing deadline remains explicitly unknown.
3. Check the citation before using an AI answer. Proof: open and inspect the source.
4. Turn a vague client request into three clarification questions. Proof: questions reference the original ambiguity.
5. Build a checklist from one approved document. Proof: reconcile each item with the source.
6. Explain an error screenshot. Proof: reproduce the error and verify the proposed fix.
7. Rewrite an email without changing the promise. Proof: compare amounts, dates and scope before and after.
8. Extract decisions and owners from fictional meeting notes. Proof: show an unassigned item as unassigned.
9. Separate facts from suggestions in an AI answer. Proof: label each against provided sources.
10. Make a reusable prompt for a repeated task. Proof: test it on a second input and show its failure case.

These are editorial briefs, not verified product feature claims. Select the actual tool after checking current documentation and capturing the workflow.

## Measurement and refinement loop

Log video ID, publication time, format, duration, topic, opening, use of characters, source URLs, production minutes and cash cost. At 24 hours and seven days capture views, engaged views, shown in feed, stayed to watch, reported AVD, average percentage viewed, subscribers gained/lost and traffic source. For long videos also record impressions, CTR and the 30-second retention point. Preserve the raw export and capture date.

Compare Shorts to Shorts and long videos to long videos. Use the same age window, similar lengths and comparable traffic sources. Changing hook, topic, duration and visual format together cannot isolate the cause. Begin with six comparable Shorts split across result-first and problem-first openings; then test brief character hosting versus direct demonstration. This is directional research, not a randomized experiment.

Working decision rule: only consider a pattern worth expanding after at least three comparable examples support it. Seek a roughly 20% relative improvement in the chosen primary metric while subscriber conversion and retention do not deteriorate. This is an internal decision threshold, not a YouTube benchmark or statistical guarantee. With fewer than 200 engaged views per video, treat percentage changes as particularly tentative and continue collecting evidence.

Low stayed-to-watch suggests revising the first frame and promise. Early retention drops suggest removing setup. Drops during the demo suggest enlarging the UI or slowing that step. Good retention with low reach suggests testing another relevant problem and packaging. Views without subscription growth suggest a weak recurring promise or poor audience fit. These are hypotheses to test, not certain diagnoses.

Review weekly: keep the best-supported problem, revise one weak production element, stop one unsupported series, and prepare the next five briefs. Record what changed and why. Do not republish near-identical videos as a substitute for a fresh test.

## Monetization route and economics

As checked November 4, YouTube lists Jordan for expanded YPP. Earlier access requires 500 subscribers, three valid public uploads in 90 days, and either 3,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months or 3 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days. At the reported 130 subscribers, the subscriber gap is 370; qualified activity gaps are unknown. This entry tier provides eligible fan funding and Shopping access, subject to feature conditions.

Ad revenue sharing currently requires 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 qualified watch hours in 12 months or 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days. Subscriber gap: 870. Shorts-feed watch time does not count toward the 4,000-hour route. Meeting counts still requires channel review and account prerequisites. Check the channel's Earn tab as the operational source of truth.

Use Shorts for discovery and test long tutorials as a potential watch-hour route. For scale only: 10 million / 90 is about 111,111 qualified Shorts views daily. At four minutes average watch duration, 4,000 hours requires about 60,000 qualifying long-video views across the relevant period. Neither calculation is a forecast; actual qualification and retention determine progress.

Suggested first-month cash ceiling: $30 total, with a $0 baseline using existing software, screen capture and properly licensed local narration. Reserve up to $10 for narration and up to $20 for specific assets only if needed and approved. These are spending envelopes, not quoted provider prices. Avoid paid promotion while learning audience fit. Track time and costs per published video. Increase spending only after repeated improvements justify the bottleneck being purchased.

Optional later revenue: a useful workflow template, disclosed affiliate links for tools actually demonstrated, or services aligned with the audience. These are separate from verified YPP monetization. Do not promise earnings or recommend a tool for commission alone.

YouTube currently announces YPP terms changes starting February 1, 2027, with updated terms acceptance by January 31 for continuing full monetization. Recheck official requirements before applying or revising forecasts.
