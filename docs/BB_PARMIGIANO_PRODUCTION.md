# BB production brief: Why aged cheese crunches

Status: approved topic, production pending. No spending or upload from this brief.

## Originality gate

Before scheduling, refresh the BB catalog and check the topic, story angle and
footage against existing uploads. A revisit needs a new angle and an original
script, footage, narration, captions and sound. A shorter cut, new title, or
different transitions alone does not qualify. Golden Fries is a private motion
reference; do not queue the recut.

## 15-second edit

- 0–3 s: Crack a wedge close to the lens. "See those white flecks? They aren't
  grains of salt." Cut on the crack; use the actual sound if available.
- 3–7 s: Show the broken face and bring the flecks into focus. "In Parmigiano
  Reggiano, those are crystals of tyrosine."
- 7–12 s: Cut to another angle as a fragment breaks off. "They form as milk
  proteins break down while the cheese ages."
- 12–15 s: Hold the broken edge for texture. "That crunch is a clue to how it aged."

Title: The Crunch Inside Parmigiano Reggiano Isn't Salt

Description: Those tiny white flecks in Parmigiano Reggiano are tyrosine
crystals. They form as the cheese matures and can add a little crunch.
Source: Parmigiano Reggiano Consortium FAQ. Add an AI illustration note if
generated visuals are used, and set the applicable Studio disclosure.

## Shoot and review

Use newly filmed footage of one wedge, or generate new shots locally once the
runtime is validated. Keep the same wedge and light direction across takes.
Show real subject movement in the first second. Make the crystals visible at
the reveal; avoid depicting them as glitter or sprinkles. Keep the narration
and captions clear of the crack sound. Check playback and the publish pack
before adding it to the queue.
